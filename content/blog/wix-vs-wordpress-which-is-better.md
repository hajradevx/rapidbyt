Every week someone asks us this question. They're starting a new website — maybe a local business, a portfolio, an online store — and they've heard of both Wix and WordPress, and they just want someone to tell them which one to pick.

The honest answer is: it depends on what you're actually trying to do. But most comparisons you'll find online are vague or sponsored by one platform. So here's a straight, no-fluff breakdown based on what we see working (and breaking) on client sites every day.

## What Each Platform Actually Is

Before comparing, it's worth being clear about what you're comparing.

**Wix** is a hosted website builder. You sign up, choose a template, drag and drop your content, and Wix handles everything else — hosting, security, updates, backups. You never touch code unless you want to. You also can't move your site to another host.

**WordPress** (specifically WordPress.org, the self-hosted version) is software you install on your own hosting. You own your files, your database, your everything. It's more work to set up, but you have complete control over every aspect of your site.

There's also WordPress.com, which is a hosted version of WordPress. It's closer to Wix in terms of simplicity but more limited than self-hosted WordPress. When people say "WordPress" in the context of building a serious site, they almost always mean the self-hosted version.

## Round 1: Ease of Use

This one isn't close. **Wix wins.**

Wix was designed from day one to be used by people with zero technical knowledge. You can have a good-looking website live in a few hours, no help needed. The editor is genuinely intuitive.

WordPress has a learning curve. You need to understand hosting, domain configuration, themes, plugins, and how they interact. The block editor (Gutenberg) is much better than it used to be, but it's still not as beginner-friendly as Wix.

If you're a solo business owner who wants to build your site yourself without learning anything technical, Wix is more realistic.

**Winner: Wix**

## Round 2: Design Flexibility

Wix gives you hundreds of templates and a flexible drag-and-drop editor. You can place elements anywhere on the page, which feels very free — until you realize that this freedom makes it harder to maintain consistency across pages, and the same freedom makes responsive design tricky. Wix has improved its mobile editor significantly, but layouts that look great on desktop sometimes need manual adjustment on mobile.

WordPress is more structured. You work within a theme's framework, which feels more constrained but produces more consistent results. With a good theme or a page builder like Elementor or Bricks, you can build almost anything you can imagine.

For unique, highly customized designs, WordPress is the better platform. For standard business sites and portfolios, Wix is perfectly capable.

**Winner: Tie (depends on your needs)**

## Round 3: SEO

This is where the comparison gets interesting, because it's the question we get most often.

Wix has improved its SEO capabilities significantly over the years. You can set custom title tags, meta descriptions, canonical URLs, and alt text. It generates sitemaps automatically. For basic SEO, it works fine.

But WordPress with Yoast SEO or Rank Math still has meaningful advantages:

- Full control over URL structure
- Custom schema markup on any page type
- More granular control over noindex, canonical, and hreflang tags
- Better handling of large sites with complex taxonomies
- Faster page speeds (more on this below)

The biggest real-world SEO difference isn't in the settings panel — it's in **page speed**. Wix sites typically score 50–70 on Google's PageSpeed Insights mobile test. A well-optimized WordPress site can consistently hit 90+. Since page speed is a ranking factor and affects [Core Web Vitals](/blog/core-web-vitals-guide-2026), this is a meaningful advantage for WordPress in competitive niches.

We've worked with businesses that switched from Wix to WordPress specifically because their Wix site couldn't pass Core Web Vitals no matter what they tried. The platform generates too much render-blocking JavaScript that you can't control.

**Winner: WordPress**

## Round 4: Performance and Speed

Wix sites have a reputation for being slow, and it's partially deserved. The platform adds a layer of overhead — its own JavaScript, its own rendering system — that you can't remove. Even on simple Wix sites, the mobile PageSpeed score often sits in the 40s or 50s.

Wix has been investing in performance improvements and has gotten better, but you're still working within their constraints. You can't switch to a faster server. You can't implement advanced caching strategies. You can't use a custom CDN configuration.

WordPress gives you full control. On good hosting (even something like Cloudflare Pages or a quality managed WordPress host), with properly optimized images, a lightweight theme, and minimal plugins, a WordPress site can be very fast. The flip side is that a poorly managed WordPress site with 40 plugins can be slower than Wix.

Performance on WordPress is a skill. If you know what you're doing, WordPress is faster. If you don't, it might not be.

**Winner: WordPress (in the right hands)**

## Round 5: E-Commerce

Wix has its own e-commerce built in. It works well for small stores — product listings, variants, payment processing, basic inventory. For a local business selling 20-50 products, Wix eCommerce is genuinely good and much simpler to set up than WooCommerce.

WordPress with WooCommerce is the most powerful e-commerce setup available outside of enterprise platforms. It handles tens of thousands of products, complex shipping rules, subscriptions, bookings, and pretty much anything else you can imagine. There are thousands of plugins specifically for WooCommerce.

But WooCommerce is complex. It requires maintenance, updates, and often a developer for anything beyond basic configuration.

For small stores: Wix is easier and good enough. For larger or more complex stores: WooCommerce wins clearly. See our detailed [Shopify vs WooCommerce comparison](/blog/shopify-vs-woocommerce-2026) for a full breakdown.

**Winner: Depends on store size — Wix for small, WooCommerce/WordPress for larger**

## Round 6: Cost

This is where most comparisons get misleading. Let's be real.

**Wix pricing (2026):**

- Free plan: very limited, shows Wix branding
- Light: ~$17/month
- Core (with e-commerce): ~$29/month
- Business: ~$36/month

All of this is paid to Wix. You don't own the hosting, and you can't move the site.

**WordPress pricing:**

- WordPress software: free
- Hosting: $5–$30/month depending on quality
- Domain: ~$12/year
- Premium theme: $50–$100 one-time (optional)
- Premium plugins: varies

On paper, WordPress can be cheaper. In practice, if you need developer help to set it up or maintain it, costs add up. If you're doing it yourself, WordPress is generally more affordable at comparable quality levels.

**Winner: WordPress for DIY, roughly comparable if you're hiring help**

## Round 7: Ownership and Portability

This is the one that people overlook and later regret.

With Wix, you cannot export your site and move it to another platform. Your content, your design, your everything lives on Wix's servers. If Wix raises prices, changes its terms, or shuts down a feature you depend on, your options are limited. We've seen businesses stuck in this situation.

With WordPress, you own everything. Your files, your database, your content. You can move to a different host in an afternoon. You can export everything. No platform lock-in.

For any business that's serious about their online presence long-term, this matters.

**Winner: WordPress (clearly)**

## So Which Should You Pick?

Here's the honest summary:

**Choose Wix if:**

- You're building a personal or small business site yourself with no technical background
- You want to be live quickly with minimal setup
- You have a small catalog (under 50 products) and want simple e-commerce
- You don't plan to heavily optimize for SEO in a competitive niche
- Simplicity matters more than control

**Choose WordPress if:**

- SEO and organic traffic is a priority
- You're building a site that will grow significantly
- You want full control over performance, design, and data
- You're in a competitive niche where Core Web Vitals matter
- You want to own your platform long-term
